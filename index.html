01 THE MISSION

Make the path in, not the noise around it.

Good propagation makes it easier for someone to move from a first question to the material that can answer it. It does not ask people to repeat claims they cannot inspect.

Use this page to choose a small contribution, find the right public resource, and communicate with enough care that curiosity can become informed participation.

THE PROPAGATION PLEDGE

“I will point to sources, separate the written from the proposed, and leave people freer to decide for themselves.”

07 THE FIVE-MOVE PLAYBOOK

One contribution can leave the signal stronger than you found it.

Use this sequence whenever you create, adapt or share something. It works at the scale of a single message or a recurring volunteer practice.

  1. 01

    Choose one public source

    Start from what is published, not what you remember or assume.

  2. 02

    Name the real question

    Write for the reader’s actual uncertainty, not a generic audience.

  3. 03

    Make one clear artifact

    A paragraph, conversation, card, translation or visual is enough when it is useful.

  4. 04

    Attach the source and scope

    State what is written, what is open and where the reader can verify it.

  5. 05

    Route the next action

    Offer a public discussion, documentation page or volunteer entry point — never a pressure tactic.

08 QUESTIONS BEFORE YOU SHARE

A little discipline protects a lot of trust.

Do I need specialised expertise to help?

No. Begin with a small, source-led contribution you can complete honestly: explain a page, make a reliable link easier to find, or welcome a question into the appropriate public place.

Can I make my own visual material?

Yes, when it is grounded in an official asset and public source. Use the brand guide, preserve the supplied mark, and make the scope of your interpretation clear.

How do I keep information current?

Link to primary sources and check the published page before repeating a claim. If you are unsure, ask a question or describe the uncertainty rather than presenting it as fact.

Where should I take an idea or question?

Use the public forum or the organization’s GitHub repositories. They make the discussion inspectable and help the right people find it.
